# LinkedIn document-ad pattern and experiment boundary

Status: pattern verified; experiment not approved
Last verified: 2026-07-20
Reference: [Notion LinkedIn example](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/playbook-1-light-yellow-playbookhtmlpdf-ugcPost-7472713714606006273-Gzad/)

## What the example is

The supplied post shows two preview pages from an 11-page PDF and then asks the member to unlock the full document. That visible behavior matches LinkedIn's documented Document Ad with Lead Gen Form flow; the permalink alone does not reveal the campaign's targeting, spend, or other private configuration:

- Document Ads are Sponsored Content, not a personal-profile paywall.
- The advertiser chooses how many pages a member can preview.
- `Unlock full document` opens a LinkedIn Lead Gen Form.
- The form exchanges member/profile information for the rest of the document.
- It does not charge a subscription or establish product access.

Sources: [LinkedIn document ads](https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a1320461), [Create document ads](https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a1325448), and [LinkedIn Lead Gen Forms](https://business.linkedin.com/en-us/marketing-solutions/native-advertising/lead-gen-ads).

## What to borrow

1. Let the reader experience real value before the transition.
2. Make the total scope visible so the preview feels like part of a substantial artifact.
3. Place one clear next action at the point of highest curiosity.
4. Keep the preview native to the feed and the commercial path simple.
5. Measure the full path rather than treating reactions or downloads as sales.

## What not to copy

1. Do not call an email/profile-data gate a `$5` paywall.
2. Do not upload the subscriber-only PDF to LinkedIn.
3. Do not put the approved free sample behind a required email form on its primary path.
4. Do not copy Notion's creative, wording, or incentive.
5. Do not treat the example's visible engagement as an expected result; its audience, spend, targeting, and distribution are not comparable evidence.

## Recommended sequence

### 1. Prove the product loop first

Finish the test-mode checkout, access, cancellation, refund, renewal, delivery, and support path. Run the 20-person founding outreach in approved waves and deliver the first Tuesday field note.

### 2. Launch organically

Use Mike's personal LinkedIn profile for the first public post. Pair a useful two- or three-page public teaser with a post-body link to the ungated Sections 1–3 sample and the `$5/month` offer. The teaser should contain a problem-led cover, one concrete framework or lesson, and a final route to the sample. The document must stand on its own; it is not the paywall. LinkedIn states that anyone who can view an organic document post can download the entire file, so it must contain no subscriber-only material. Source: [Upload and share documents on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a518909/upload-and-share-documents-on-linkedin?lang=en).

### 3. Run a paid Document Ad only if approved

Use a website-visits or website-conversions objective rather than the example's Lead Gen Form for the first paid test:

1. Source document: the approved public sample or a purpose-built public excerpt.
2. Preview: enough opening pages to demonstrate quality without uploading subscriber-only content.
3. Destination: the instrumented public sample/sales path.
4. CTA: one action leading to that destination.
5. Payment and entitlement: the external `$5/month` checkout and subscriber system.
6. Attribution: a unique UTM source/campaign and source-level purchase event.

This preserves the pattern while keeping the free sample free and the paid product truly paid.

### 4. Treat lead generation as a different experiment

If a Lead Gen Form is tested later, use a separate public lead magnet and state the exchange clearly. Route the thank-you page to the public sample or sales page. Never grant subscriber access from a form submission.

## Experiment scorecard

Hypothesis: a controlled native preview will move qualified LinkedIn readers into the public sample and produce confirmed `$5/month` purchases at an acceptable acquisition cost.

Measure:

1. Approved spend and impressions.
2. Preview opens and completion.
3. CTA clicks and qualified sample visits.
4. Checkout starts.
5. Confirmed purchases.
6. Customer acquisition cost.
7. Refunds, cancellations, and access failures by source.

Do not count reactions, downloads, or Lead Gen Form submissions as paid conversions.

## Gates and stop conditions

- No ad before the complete purchase-to-access path passes.
- No ad without a fixed budget, named owner, Page/ad-account permissions, approved creative, approved destination, and verified analytics.
- Stop acquisition for any critical payment, access, cancellation, or delivery failure.
- Stop or revise the test when spend reaches its approved cap; do not increase budget without a new decision.
- Record evidence separately from organic personal-profile results.

## Open decision

Decide whether the Document Ad is only a pattern reference or a paid post-founding-cohort experiment. If it becomes an experiment, confirm the LinkedIn Page, Campaign Manager access, objective, and budget before asset production.
